After the second meeting, I have a good direction on my design. To summarize, I need to 
design and fabricate a light and fast acting gripper with decent gripping strength and
sensitive response to objects in hand.

My first requirement is getting a good assembly with lead screws, NEMA17 stepper motors,
PD stepper motor driver, and create the housing that is light enough to be universally
accessible to manipulators with ability to carry a decent payload. During the assembly,
I noticed a few issues. Using lead screws create extremely high gear ratios that can 
enable a 0.4Nm torque stepper motor to apply forces orders higher than the datasheet.
But this came with the cost of not being able to be backdrivable. This means that a 
foreign entity cannot force the gripper open or control the NEMA17 actuator. Another 
cost was the overall lead screw. To actuate an open or close on the gripper, I needed at
least 50mm in addition to the length of the NEMA17 and PD stepper module. All combined, 
the overall length of the end-effector is much larger making the TCP significantly 
further from the final joint of the manipulator. And the final constrain, the sensorless
homing of the TMC2209 module calculates backEMF generation. This means if the counter-
rotation stall applied upon the jaws of the gripper is very slow, there is minimal backEMF
which would erroneously be ignored by the system causing unexpected object release/slipage.

The combination of these constraints resulted in a gripper that can't dynamically apply 
grip strength, nor can it be a universal end-effector. I must pivot and find an in between
that has good position feedback like that of an encoded stepper motor, high torque of a
servo motor, and a current based position sensor. That's when I stumbled upon the Dynamixel
series advanced continuous rotation servo motors. I should speak with Dr. Judhi about this!
