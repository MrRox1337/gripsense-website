From my 6th meeting with Dr. Judhi, I decided to continue the work on the enclosure.
I began by first using the 3D model of the XM430-W210-T actuator and design around
it to make the new rack and pinion enclosure. Fortunately, it was only a matter of 
a few extrudes, cuts, fillets, and chamfers to make a viable version 2 of the gripper
enclosure.

After 3D printing and testing the slider performance, I finally mounted the actuator
onto the gripper with screws and moved onto researching evaluation metrics. I thought
of a few approaches. The most intuitive method would be to use a Force Sensitive
Resistor (FSR) but they don't come with calibrated values; if the object in contact 
has a limited surface area smaller than the FSR, then I can't even get true values.

So with precalibration in mind and the requirement of being able to measure weight
differences in grams, the most reliable method would be to use a strain gauge. An 
everyday item that uses strain gauge for gram-level accuracy would be a kitchen weight
scale so I decided to use this for my grip strength evaluation.

With the enclosure optimized and evaluation metrics decided, I need to start working
on the code that can help me test the Dynamixel actuator. I first found that it is 
very easy to use the Dynamixel Wizard 2.0 for identifying the actuator and tune its 
internal details like limits for voltage, temperature, current, position, torque, and
velocity while also change details like ID, baud rate, and control method and also
evaluate motion based on velocity, position, current, and torque. 

Now it is only a matter of the U2D2 module to arrive. Until then I should at least build
a power supply for the actuator since the XM430-W210-T module works at 11.1V.
