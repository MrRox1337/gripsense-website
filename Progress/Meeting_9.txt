The meeting this week was online. It was mainly through messaging and correspondences.
I wanted to give Dr. Judhi an update on the codebase but without concrete evidence of
execution, I could not show anything. So I decided to get some initial test done and
evaluate some combinations for slip and grip test.

I ran the tests for all finger materials at no padding and rubberband padding. My 
first observation was at calibration where both PETG and PLA had a delay after pulling
back at minimum open to return to maximum open. I also noticed that TPU had the fastest
calibration compared to all the hard materials. During data collection, I suspected TPU
to be the best performer at grabbing components but PLA was the best at no paddin
followed by PETG, TPU, and finally ABS. ABS failed at the first test of closure due to
delamination but I also suspect that it is not a controlled test since this filament
was printed on another device making the fabrication environment inconsistent compared
to the other materials.

PLA filament is extremely hard, yet brittle, but this permitted the gripper to apply 
maximum closing force at the end-effector TCP and TPU had the most inconsistent 
readings for slip test for the same reasons.

When I reported these initial findings to Dr. Judhi, he funnily commented that I pushed
myself very hard this week and I should take a break (hehe). He mentioned that testing
the grip quality using different material and pads is the best move but if I am comparing
the strength of the pinch of different materials (often called Force Closure in robotics),
I need to test the pinches without padding, then choose the best material, and finally do
padding based grip quality check.

I still believe I can make some meaningful discoveries if I continue collecting all 480
