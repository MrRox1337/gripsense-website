From my discussions with Dr. Judhi in the 4th meeting, I learned two things: firstly,
the TCP is variable and I would need to create a mathematical formula to calculate the
TCP based on the width of the manipulated object. Secondly, this is pretty complex and
I haven't even created the parallel gripper.

I decided to put the TCP calculation to the side and focus on creating the second design,
the parallel gripper. To build this, a core difference is that the gripper is not directly
actuated by the servo, instead it is actuated by a rack-and-pinion assembly. I still 
maintained backdrivability as the wide servo grippers so I still have the ability to sense 
object in hand.

I found a FeatureScript that creates the rack-and-pinion assembly and used the base model
to create the full assembly on OnShape. Here I created special rails, along which the rack
can slide whitout derailing. I also ensured there is plenty of space for the motor to be 
mounted for the first design and added some ribs along the fingers to ensure internal
structural strength.

Before the week ended, I started the 3D print of this model as it looked good enough for the
first iteration.
