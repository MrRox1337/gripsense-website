After my 7th meeting, I was happy to proceed to designing the full end-of-arm tooling.
The U2D2 module arrived on time so I began testing the Dynamixel actuator. I noticed
that by applying varying limits, I can clamp the maximum range, velocity, and torque.
And by doing so, I could continue to get the full motion of GripSense while not harming
the gripping fingers or the object I manipulate.

With the Current-based position sensor method, I tested the grip strength at the weakest
current limit below which the actuator will fail to operate the fingers. The gripper 
applied about 850 grams of gripping strength measured on the strain gauge kitchen scale.
At a current limit over 130 resolution units, I snapped one of the fingers due to excessive
forces applied by a higher torque limit. At 120 units, I was able to apply about 1.5 Kg of
gripping strength.

So I decided to range between the minimum operable current limit of 90 resolution units to
a maximum of 120 units for testing GripSense. But this was only because I used Transparent
PETG filament to make the fingers. I decided to see what would happen if I make fingers of
different material as my evaluation metrics for the quality and robustness of GripSense.

With that in mind, I started prints for more fingers made from PETG, PLA, ABS, and TPU and
I will present the idea to Dr. Judhi to get his input.
