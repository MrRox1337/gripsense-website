With the test plan confirmed and real numbers in hand, this week I switched over to the
final writeup. I restructured the paper into Methodology, split into Mechanical Design and
Control Software, with a merged Discussion and a proper Testing and Results section instead
of the placeholder that had been sitting there.

I then split the requirements into two groups. The functional ones cover grasp and release,
bounded force, object-in-hand, slip, and status reporting. The non-functional ones cover
force safety, no added distal sensor, backdrivability, standard integration, and software
portability. I labelled them FR-i to FR-v and NR-i to NR-v using my own shorthand rather
than some generated numbering scheme, since I need to be able to point at them from memory
in the viva.

Then I wrote the Smart Servo Selection section as an argument by elimination. Actuators with
no feedback fail outright. Actuators with position-only feedback, like encoded DC motors,
steppers, and hobby servos, sense nothing usable for grasp state and also run their current
uncontrolled at nominal-or-stall with no torque bound, which is a safety problem as much as
a sensing one. From there the argument narrows to current-based position control in general
and then to the XM430-W210-T specifically, on gear train durability, headroom below its
current ceiling, and its built-in thermal, velocity, position, and current protection that
works independent of whatever my driver software does. I also transposed the Actuation
Comparison table so the actuator classes are rows instead of columns, which lets it fit in a
single IEEE column rather than spanning both.

The last thing I did was cut Picker-Bot down from five name-drops to one citation.
Everywhere else the requirements now derive from the application domain itself, meaning
rigid low-friction educational circuit modules, rather than from "Picker-Bot needs X". This
makes the paper read as its own contribution with one clearly cited piece of prior work
instead of a Picker-Bot appendix. While doing this I caught a real bug where a second
mention of Shang et al. had the author name written in prose but no cite key behind it,
which would have rendered as a bare name with no IEEE citation number.
